# Explain, teach and summarize actual work

Use when an explanation, learning plan, technical document or work recap is requested.
This method does not replace authorized implementation with a lesson.

## Understanding and practice

Choose the recipient's relevant understanding goal. Start with the smallest complete
definition and concrete mechanism, then add structure only as useful. For example,
explain a save's request/result before adding its worker and retry edge. No fixed
diagram count, unsolicited quiz or inferred seniority is required.

For an explicit learning roadmap, establish baseline experience and time constraints,
choose achievable milestones, deliberate-practice exercises and observable evidence
criteria. Let the learner do the requested exercise. A retrospective compares their
actual explanation/work with the milestone, identifies a weak concept and adjusts
reinforcement or pacing. Observed generated code alone cannot establish human
learning, expertise or career level. A routine implementation needs no curriculum.

## Technical documents

Choose whether the reader needs a tutorial, task-oriented how-to, reference or
explanation. Separate conflicting purposes when they obstruct use; do not force
four sections on every document or PR. Put prerequisites and warnings before the
action they guard. At meaningful procedural steps name the expected visible result
and stop/recovery condition. Preserve real command names, use one term per concept,
and never invent output for an unexecuted step. Link background rather than making
the reader search an essay for the next command.

## Work recaps and pending decisions

Resolve exact dates/timezone, selected projects and authorship before querying.
Inspect actual diffs and outcomes, including failed/reverted attempts, rather than
listing commit titles or attributing a team's work to one author. Group useful
results as fixes, maintenance or new behavior where meaningful. Committed, merged,
released and deployed remain distinct; no inferred motivation/performance judgment
or Git identity change is needed for read-only history.

A catch-up includes every still-pending human decision in the selected scope,
including earlier unresolved calls. Keep agent-executable checks out of the human
queue. Give the decision, weighed options, recommendation and material evidence,
unknowns and risks in the response; a bare link is insufficient. Do not invent a
choice merely to fill a template. A requested video uses
[video production](../../social-preview/references/video.md), not a storyboard claim.

## Change history and retained infrastructure

Ground release attribution in the actual diff and introducing history, preserving
source notices and contribution ownership. A recent touch is not necessarily the
introducing change. An empty permanent experiment or migration framework can be a
retained extension boundary after feature graduation; inspect owning callers and
future contract before calling it dead code. Dependency-report tasks stay report
only and preserve explicitly excluded major changes; an explanation is not update
or publication authority.
