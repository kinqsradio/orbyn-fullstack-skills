# Synthetic product decision: a manual correction before a new feature

A team requests an AI renewal reminder. Three support reports say users missed a
renewal date. Those reports establish a problem worth examining, not its frequency
across all users or the value of an AI solution. No interviews or analytics were
performed for this synthetic example.

Inspect the current journey and known evidence: the existing date field is hidden
behind an ambiguous label, and an ordinary calendar export is already available.
Compare no change, a clearer label/help path and manual calendar use, a deterministic
reminder, and an AI assistant. There is no observed need for generated content or
autonomous scheduling yet. Choose the smaller discoverability correction and keep
AI as an unproven alternative rather than adding a provider and background system.

A consented usability session could ask representative users to find the date and
explain how they would track it, without naming the intended button. Define useful
completion, assistance required and misunderstanding before observation. Passing
that task would support usability; it would not prove fewer missed renewals.

If later outcome measurement is authorized, define an eligible renewal cohort,
observed missed-renewal baseline, window and support/accessibility guardrails first.
Use existing privacy-respecting evidence. Uncontrolled changes in support volume
cannot establish causality. Revisit a deterministic reminder if the workflow issue
persists; consider AI only for a demonstrated need it can serve better.

Acceptance for the small patch remains its source/UI contract and designated
checks. Do not invent experiment results, treat three reports as a population
estimate, enable telemetry, or block that bounded correction on a growth program.
